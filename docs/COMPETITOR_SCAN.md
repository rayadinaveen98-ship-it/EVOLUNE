# Competitor scan — v0.1 (public feature comparison)

**Date:** 2026-11-10. **Method:** First-party product and help pages; this is a preliminary **feature scan**, not a validated market-size study, user review synthesis, or hands-on product test. Prices, metrics and features can change.

| Product | Publicly described focus | Useful observation for EVOLUNE |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Finch](https://finchcare.com/about-finch) | Self-care pet; goals, reflection, breathing, adventures, optional premium | Companion + intrinsic encouragement can be inviting. Our identity must not simply clone a pet, shop, or growth reward loop. |
| [Fabulous](https://help.thefabulous.co/en/support/solutions/articles/101000427430) | Science-framed routines, 3–12-week journeys, 5–30-day challenges, coaching | The category already has structured challenge programs. We need clearer user authorship, honesty about evidence and individualized recovery, if validated. |
| [Daylio](https://apps.apple.com/in/app/daylio-journal-mood-tracker/id1194023242) | Mood/activity journaling, statistics, goals, trend exploration | Quick logging and longitudinal patterns are table stakes in self-tracking. Avoid turning subjective mood-energy correlation into causal claims. |
| [stoic.](https://www.getstoic.com/features) | Guided journaling and morning/evening reflection | Reflection has an established product category; keep our reflection quick, optional and actionable. |
| [Routinery](https://www.routinery.app/features) | Step-by-step routine timer, mood tracking, widgets and integrations | Strong execution tools exist; do not create a timer-heavy routine app by default. |
| [Habitica](https://habitica.com/) | RPG-style tasks, rewards and punishments | A deliberately quieter, non-punitive model is a meaningful design contrast, but still requires user validation. |

## Working positioning hypothesis

**EVOLUNE helps users design personally meaningful challenges and learn how to sustain them; reflection and recovery are first-class experiences, not afterthoughts.**

This is a **hypothesis**, not an evidence-backed differentiation claim. Features like journaling, recovery, reminders and personalization already exist in various competitors; the goal is to create a better *integrated experience*, not pretend those features are new.

## Unvalidated opportunities to test with users
1. The user prefers one personally chosen action over a large habit catalog at initial onboarding.
2. Recovery/adjustment feels more constructive than losing a streak.
3. A finite challenge with a continuation decision is clearer than indefinite compliance pressure.
4. Beautiful growth art adds emotional resonance without creating extra compulsive checking.
5. Simple, source-linked explanations increase trust.

## Suggested early research (before implementation)
- 8–12 exploratory interviews across users at different stages of personal-growth experience (research target, not achieved).
- Short observation tasks with 2–3 competitor products, respecting licenses and privacy.
- Prototype tests: create one challenge, complete a day, miss several days, review at milestone, restart or continue.
- Document evidence, unmet needs, and costs separately from aspirational visuals.

## Safety/ethics
Do not optimize for user guilt, forced streaks, or compulsive attention. Do not suggest our product treats mental-health conditions. Avoid collecting health, mood, and journal content without a defined consent and data protection model. Avoid copying competitors' art or UX assets.
