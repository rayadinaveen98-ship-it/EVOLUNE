# Scientific evidence register — v0.1

**Purpose:** Distinguish external research evidence from the untested efficacy of EVOLUNE itself. All feature recommendations remain hypotheses until evaluated with real users. Reviewed 2026-11-10.

## E01 — Habit formation varies; 21 days is not a universal threshold
**Source:** Singh B, Murphy A, Maher C, Smith AE. *Time to Form a Habit: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis of Health Behaviour Habit Formation and Its Determinants.* Healthcare (2024). https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39685110/ ; full text https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11641623/  
**Finding:** 20 studies / 2,601 participants; the subset of studies reporting times found medians ~59–66 days, means ~106–154 days, and wide variation (4–335 days). Most included studies were rated at high risk of bias (11/20), so avoid treating the numbers as a universal prediction.  
**Product implication:** 7-, 21-, and 30-day challenges are **review milestones**, not habit-certification deadlines. Support longer-term follow-through and individual choices.  
**Evidence use:** Direct evidence for variable habit formation; not proof that EVOLUNE works.

## E02 — Implementation intentions / if-then action plans
**Source:** Gollwitzer PM, Sheeran P. *Implementation Intentions and Goal Achievement: A Meta-analysis of Effects and Processes.* Advances in Experimental Social Psychology (2006). https://doi.org/10.1016/S0065-2601(06)38002-1  
**Finding:** Meta-analysis of 94 independent tests reported a positive effect on goal attainment (d=.65). Effects depend on intervention contexts; a specific mobile implementation remains to be tested.  
**Product implication:** Offer an optional concrete plan (cue, action, and contingency), including obstacles, not just an abstract goal.  
**Evidence use:** Supports the technique; not a guarantee of results for an individual.

## E03 — Behavior change technique taxonomy
**Source:** Michie S et al. *The Behavior Change Technique Taxonomy (v1) of 93 Hierarchically Clustered Techniques.* Annals of Behavioral Medicine (2013). https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23512568/  
**Finding:** 93 behavior-change techniques in 16 groups; a structured language for describing intervention ingredients.  
**Product implication:** Tag proposed interventions and design reviews explicitly (e.g., goal-setting, action planning, self-monitoring, feedback, reviewing goals).  
**Evidence use:** **Taxonomy is not efficacy evidence** that all 93 techniques work or that including them makes a product effective.

## Design hypotheses requiring validation
- A compassion-based 'resume' flow may help user re-engagement more than punitive streak resets.
- Optional mood/energy check-ins may help reflection without creating pressure.
- Calm 3D growth environments may increase perceived meaning without increasing distraction.
- Choosing small, achievable behaviors may improve user experience and completion.

These are research/design hypotheses, **not established clinical outcomes**. Verify through interviews, usability testing, and appropriately designed evaluations.

## Evidence governance
For every science-derived feature record:
1. Claim and the exact population/behavior it applies to.
2. Source (DOI/URL, publication date, study type, sample, limitations).
3. Proposed mechanism and precise app behavior.
4. Potential harm (pressure, inappropriate medical inference, privacy exposure).
5. Status: Supported technique / Inference / Untested hypothesis / Rejected.
6. What would confirm or falsify the feature's usefulness in the app.

## Health and safety boundaries
This is a general self-development/wellness concept, not a diagnostic or therapeutic product. Do not imply preventing, treating, or curing medical or psychiatric conditions. Keep any mental-health exercises voluntary and clearly contextualised. Collect sensitive reflections minimally and provide meaningful delete/export controls before launch. Ensure content review and appropriate crisis-resource handling if later mental-health features are added.
