# End-to-end experience journeys — v0.1

**Status:** Design narratives and testable behaviors, not final UI or development completion.

## Journey A — 'I want to make a start'
**Context:** An adult overwhelmed by dozens of habits.
1. Open Evolune and choose 'Start with one thing' or 'Explore my growth.'
2. Optionally select Growth Area: Body & energy. User writes 'I want calmer mornings.'
3. Choose one action: 'Walk for 10 minutes after breakfast.'
4. Select 21 calendar days, 5 days/week, optional reminder, backup 2-minute walk.
5. Preview the actual number of planned opportunities, with date boundaries.
6. Today screen shows the next action with a tiny, clear call to action.
**Success:** creation is understandable in a research target of ~3 minutes; user can change details without starting again. No forced account or mood disclosure.

## Journey B — 'I missed four days'
**Context:** A person returns after interruption.
1. Today shows factual status without an accusatory alert.
2. Continue their scheduled action immediately, or select a calm 'Adjust plan' path.
3. Optional prompt: 'What got in the way?' with practical categories and freeform skip.
4. Choose unchanged plan, smaller action, schedule update, or temporary pause.
5. Original records are not erased. Future scheduled opportunities reflect any deliberate revision.
**Success:** resume is possible in <=2 purposeful steps; missed days remain truthful; 'pause' cannot be misread as completed.

## Journey C — 'What did my 21 days mean?'
**Context:** 21 calendar-day milestone reached, not necessarily 21 sessions.
1. Summary distinguishes calendar time, due opportunities, completed, partial, rested, missed and paused.
2. Optional reflection asks what helped, what was difficult, and how effort felt.
3. No claim that the user permanently formed a habit.
4. Offer choices: keep going, adjust, begin another challenge, finish and celebrate, or archive.
**Success:** user can describe progress without assuming a misleading streak or number.

## Journey D — 'I want private reflection'
**Context:** User journals about a personal experience.
1. Reflection is optional and can be untied to any challenge.
2. Clear privacy explanation, no undisclosed sharing.
3. User can see/edit/delete their entries, export them, and later select local/sync privacy choices once engineering is designed.
4. Insights must not diagnose emotional or psychiatric conditions.
**Success:** the user understands where their data goes and can erase or export it. Encryption/backup specifics need technical design; do not claim they exist.

## First prototype deliverables (not yet produced)
- Navigation map showing destination hierarchy and one-focus principle.
- Low-fidelity interaction steps for all four journeys.
- Two/three user-approved **visual directions**, not one imposed style.
- High-fidelity Android screens with functional options, not decorative mockups only.
- Test script for each edge case and small-screen accessibility review.

## Research questions before design lock
- Do first-time users prefer a quick-start challenge or a structured personal profile?
- Is one focus calming, or does it feel artificially limiting?
- How should users understand partial completion and fallback actions?
- Is an illustrated living world emotionally helpful, or too gamified?
- Which personal-growth areas feel natural and inclusive across users?
- What is the minimum helpful reflection without burden?
